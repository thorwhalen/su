su.words#

Word-timestamped transcription of speech, including speech under music.

transcribe_words(audio) runs these steps, each a keyword-argument seam with a working default:

  1. decode — any container, through su.casting.to_waveform() (ffmpeg via pydub; bytes and file-likes never need to touch disk), at its own sample rate.

  2. separate — keep the voice, drop the instrument: Demucs htdemucs’s vocals stem (demucs_separator()), at the model’s rate. separation=False skips it, for speech without music.

  3. detect_speech — where someone speaks: Silero VAD at threshold 0.3, with tight region edges (silero_speech_regions()).

  4. recognise — faster-whisper large-v3 (int8) with word timestamps, **no initial prompt** (Whisper copies a prompt into silent stretches) and condition_on_previous_text=False (whisper_recogniser()).

  5. drop — segments that are Whisper’s known hallucinations (“Subtitles by Amara”, long runs of one character) are set apart (is_hallucination()).

  6. align — torchaudio’s MMS_FA forced alignment, per segment, moves each word’s boundaries to its 20 ms frame (mms_aligner()). A spoken capital letter is aligned by its name (“B” as “bee”). An alignment that lands far from Whisper’s time is not trusted. alignment=False keeps Whisper’s times.

The result (WordTranscript) keeps Whisper’s own word times beside the aligned ones, the speech regions, the speech regions no word covers (“untranscribed speech”), and the dropped segments, so a caller can judge what to trust.

The default models are made once per process and reused across calls; pass your own seams to control that.

Needs the transcribe extra (demucs, faster-whisper, torch, torchaudio) and ffmpeg. All heavy imports are lazy, so import su.words costs nothing.

Known limit: a single spoken letter under a chord stays unreliable (runs vary between reruns); use such words as anchors, and label from another signal where it matters.

Example

>>> t = transcribe_words("session.m4a")
>>> [(w.text, round(w.start, 2)) for w in t.words][:3]
[('I', 0.52), ("I'm", 0.6), ('signing', 0.82)]

su.words.demucs_separator(*, model='htdemucs', stem='vocals', device=None)[source]#

A separator keeping one Demucs stem (default: htdemucs’s vocals).

It takes audio at any rate, separates at the model’s rate (44.1 kHz for htdemucs) and returns the stem at 16 kHz. Runs on MPS or CUDA when available. The model is loaded on first use.

su.words.is_hallucination(text)[source]#

Whether a segment’s text is one of Whisper’s known hallucinations.

That is: nothing but punctuation, exactly a known credit phrase, or a long run of one repeated character or token. Real speech that merely contains a phrase, or a short repetition (“no no no”, “la la la la”), is kept.

>>> is_hallucination("Subtitles by Amara.")
True
>>> is_hallucination("ささささささささささ")
True
>>> is_hallucination("Please subscribe to my channel, and today we cook rice.")
False
>>> is_hallucination("no no no no")
False
Return type:

bool

su.words.mms_aligner(*, device=None, pad=0.2, max_shift=1.0)[source]#

An aligner using torchaudio’s MMS_FA (multilingual, romanised a–z).

Each segment is aligned on its own audio, padded by pad seconds. A word with no alignable letters keeps Whisper’s times, and so does one whose aligned start lands more than max_shift seconds from Whisper’s (a forced alignment always finds some place). A segment whose alignment fails keeps Whisper’s times and is logged. The model is loaded on first use.

Return type:

Callable[[ndarray, int, Sequence[Segment]], list[Segment]]

su.words.spoken_form(word)[source]#

The letters MMS_FA aligns for a word.

Accents are folded, then lower-case a–z and apostrophes are kept; a capital letter on its own (other than “A”) becomes its name.

>>> spoken_form("B"), spoken_form("I'm,"), spoken_form("café"), spoken_form("a")
('bee', "i'm", 'cafe', 'a')
>>> spoken_form("42")
''
